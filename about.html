About the studio

Founded on a
single wheel,
in 2016.

Kiln & Co. started as one potter's wheel in a garage and grew into a nine-wheel studio, two kilns, and a teaching program that has now graduated over eight hundred students.

Our story

Slow work, on purpose

Founder Mara Odell began throwing pots as a way to slow down after a decade in restaurant kitchens. What began as weekend classes for friends became a studio built around the same idea: good work takes the time it takes. We still fire in small batches, still teach in groups small enough to know everyone's name, and still believe a wobbly first bowl is worth celebrating.

How a piece is made

Five turns from clay to shelf

Every piece that leaves the studio passes through the same five stages, whether it's a student's first cup or a hundred-piece restaurant order.

  1. 01 — Wedge

    Prepare the clay

    Air is worked out of the clay by hand to keep it from cracking in the kiln.

  2. 02 — Center

    Set on the wheel

    The clay is centered so it spins true before any shape is pulled.

  3. 03 — Throw

    Pull the form

    Walls are raised and shaped, then left to stiffen overnight.

  4. 04 — Bisque

    First firing

    An eight-hour firing hardens the piece so it can hold glaze.

  5. 05 — Glaze

    Glaze & fire again

    Glaze is applied by hand and the piece returns to the kiln at cone 6.

What we hold onto

Studio values

  • —Small classes, always capped at six seats, so every student gets wheel time with an instructor.
  • —Local clay and glaze materials sourced from suppliers within two hundred miles.
  • —Open studio access for members, seven days a week, no booking required.
  • —Every commission fired in-house, in the same kilns our students use.
